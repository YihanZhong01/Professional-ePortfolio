Public Health Leadership Narrative

About me

My path into public health began with an unanswered question. For my undergraduate thesis, I studied atrial fibrillation patients with a CHA₂DS₂-VASc score of 1, a group that trials rarely include and guidelines leave unclear. I learned that with real-world data, an answer is only as trustworthy as the methods behind it.

At Cornell, I brought health education to people held at Tompkins County Jail, a population left out of most health programs. Designing for this setting taught me to listen to partners, speak plainly, and follow through.

Next, I plan to pursue a PhD in pharmacoepidemiology to build evidence for the populations that standard research overlooks.

01 · Pharmacoepidemiology research

Anticoagulation in low-risk atrial fibrillation

Bachelor's thesis · First Affiliated Hospital of Ningbo University · 2024 – 2025

Should AF patients with a non-sex CHA₂DS₂-VASc score of 1 take oral anticoagulants? Trials rarely study this group, so guidelines give no clear answer. This retrospective cohort study tested whether anticoagulation improved their prognosis.

My role. Refined inclusion criteria with cardiologists, screened confounders (adherence, cost, time to treatment), cleaned cohort data, and ran Kaplan–Meier survival analysis.

  • Confounding Control
  • Survival Analysis

Smoking & Education Association Explorer

Data Analysis with R (VTPEH 6270) · Spring 2026

An interactive Shiny dashboard on 2024 BRFSS data. Users switch chart types and education groupings, and statistics update with each filter. Smoking fell from 22.7% to 8.1% as education rose (low vs. high: OR 3.34, 95% CI 3.23–3.45).

Consent and Boundaries Cards for Autistic Teens

Health Communication project · Fall 2026 · In progress

A bilingual card set for parents in Shanghai to use with autistic teens aged 13–15. Eight everyday scenarios model consent and personal boundaries, then show a safe response to practice. Based on Social Cognitive Theory and UNESCO guidance.
